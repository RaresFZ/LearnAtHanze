## Slide 4

Manage your SPO
The goal should be that a SPO/SRO relationship achieves more that just what’s in the contract. So there could also be non-contractual goals.
DO’s:
Utilize the strengths of the SPO
Monitor the SPO to prevent its weaknesses to become apparent
Treat SPO personnel as your own colleagues
Get to know and align with the SPO’s key responsible people (especially the horizontals), not only the service managers and alike..

## Slide 5

Manage your SPO: Good practices
Organisation awareness: Understand the full structure of the SPO: reward innovation (ideas) of the SPO in favour of the SRO.
Optimize production, in quality and timeliness. DON’t: say yes to the SRO’s wishes if this is not realistic out of fear of losing business. DO: make realistic agreements.
HRM: recognize any employees performance, the SRO employees as well as the SPO’s employees. Reward high performing employees as well financially as in personal recognition (by higher management)

## Slide 6

Manage your SPO: Good practices
Attrition targets: reward attrition rates below 10%
Innovation targets: It helps to set innovation targets for an SRO, e.g. demand 10 innovations from an SRO each year. Also facilitate the SRO’s in their efforts to achieve these innovations (e.g. a dedicated support team)
Pricing models:
Fixed price contract (an agreed upon set of activities for a fixed price)
Time and Materials contract (pay staff per hour and for materials)
Staff augmentation (hire professional staff)
All kinds of variations and combinations can be made in a contract.

## Slide 7

Manage your SPO: Good practices
Knowledge management
Hiring an SPO is a risk, concerning knowledge (!vendor lock-in). An SRO has to take care that knowledge (about infrastructure, software, documentation and procedures) is held inside in case the SPO leaves. This requires measures such as:
Keep SRO personnel on key positions
Agree on a minimum % (e.g. 25%) SRO personnel
Business continuity knowledge retention testing: test if SRO personnel can do the work without SPO personnel

## Slide 8

Manage your SPO: Good practices
Pyramid Management
There should be a balance in unexperienced and experienced employees, like a pyramid. A few with a lot of experience (> 10 years) to a lot of personnel with few experience  (0-3 years), called ‘freshers’. For tough jobs you need a heavy pyramid (more experience), for light jobs you can apply a lean pyramid.
In a contract, agreements should be made about the pyramid: the balance of experienced and unexperienced employees.

## Slide 9

Manage your SPO: Good practices
Productivity
Productivity must be managed and expressed in quantifiable portions. Function points for development jobs, # of incidents, changes, number of managed software applications, # managed hardware objects etc…
Contracts are very important to make (operational) agreements and set expectations from the start to prevent problems. 
In the day to day operation, a lot of things turn out to be complex and solving problems together is more important than stick to contract rules. Only use the contract in case of escalation or as big stick.

## Slide 10

Your own oversees IT Department: Yes or No?
Yes: 
lower cost than an IT department in your headquarter office
keep knowledge inside
still keep focus on the core business at home
No, unless…:
Captives (your own external department) tend to be considered as cost centers, back-offices, not to be recognized enough by the headquarters, so often low empowerment and low motivation are created. Also cultural differences add to this. Face to face interactions have appeared to be essential! Letting employees and management travel across is worth the money! Just taking orders from the headquarters leads to demotivation. Collaboration between both organisations leads to motivation.

## Slide 11

Stay independent: Exit Management !
At the start of an outsourcing relationship, also prepare the exit!
Scope and duration. What part of the business is terminated (usually 100%) . You could make a differentiated plan for different services. How long does each exit period take.
Governance during the exit period: This must be treated as a project.
What are the deliverables. What documentation is necessary for the successor to take over.
Employee transfer clause: some employees on key positions might be needed to transfer
Knowledge transition: determine how knowledge must be transferred. E.g. by shadowing for a certain period.
Intellectual property: determine who owns intellectual property that is developed during the relationship.
Financial terms: How and for what is the SPO being paid during the exit procedure.
Cooperation: During the exit phase, al lot of unforeseen situations will occur. Agreements must be made that the incumbent SPO will cooperate in these matters.
Exit plan testing: the agreed exit situation (project situation) will be tested (this could be done in a staged way) on non-core business days, e.g. once every 2 years.

Price
Definition of the services
What personnel profile
KPI’s: Service availability % (could be varied between services)
Measures to prevent vendor lock-in
Assuring specific requirements
Innovation
Report content
Compensation arrangements (e.g. a fine of compensation if requirements are not met)
Procedures (requests, changes (updates etc.), complaints, projects)
! measures to prevent the pitfalls mentioned in the Lecture
