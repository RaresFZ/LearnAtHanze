## Slide 4

Definition
Disruptive technology:  a technical innovation that improves a product  of service in ways that the market does not expect , typically by being lower priced or designed for a different set of consumers or a different market place.
Outperforming traditional ways with new technology  (e.g. SSD, ChatGPT)
Superior performance (Apple, Tesla)
Lower cost (Ryan Air, Aldi, Action)
New business models (Dell computers, Amazon, Netflix, Spotify, Airbnb)

## Slide 5

What aspect is innovative?
Innovation can be applied on many aspects: 
product, (primary) e.g.  Apple, Tesla
process, (supporting) e.g.Toyota production process (LEAN principles), Dell, Alibaba
new combinations of existing elements. E.g. Coffeepads, E-bikes, agricultural drones, smart watch, hybrid cars..
Innovation is relative!

## Slide 6

The market approach
Low end disruption: because established companies compete by adding more features at a (too) high pace (they aren’t even used) , opportunities arise at the low end by making simple and cheap products (Aldi, Ryan Air, IKEA) (no new technology!)
New market disruption: extending an innovation to a new market (Ebay, Uber, E-bike). (new technology! many times using internet or mobile technology in existing markets)
Top down; new products that outperform the existing ones, sold (initially) at a higher price. (Apple, Tesla, PlayStation, Samsung)

## Slide 7

Strategies to deal with disruptive innovations
New entrants have competitive opportunities using new technologies;  are able to enter existing markets or explore new markets. 
How should existing companies deal with this?
Existing companies can ‘defend’ themselves by modernizing (e.g. putting their services to internet) their old services or products (‘sustaining innovations’). E.g. chainstores start webshops
