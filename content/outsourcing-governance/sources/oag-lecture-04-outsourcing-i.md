## Slide 9

One best structure?
Organisational structures should be aligned with the (changing) strategies! Otherwise they could end up being very inefficient.
So an organisational structure that is very efficient in one situation can be very inefficient in another. 
For example, after a take-over or merger, you must change the structure. Structure is more than a drawing, responsibilities and hierarchy;  In practice it is a web of agreements and practices between people (with characters) about positions, procedures, processes, cultures and technologies! It must be redesigned in any new situation and changed strategy!

## Slide 11

What is a good IT Provider (SPO)?
It appears to be that TOP IT SPO’s replicate their behaviour to their customers, also pushing them to greater achievements. 
There are two key pillars for success: 
competent, loyal and motivated employees 
and specialized (on the customers business) unique services.

## Slide 12

Motivated empoyees
One way to motivate personnel is considered to be a fair employee appraisal system. High performance, high bonus.
Employee motivation is also indicated by the attrition rate 
(= % of employees that vacate or move out over a specified time frame)
Low attrition rates indicate better motivated personnel. 

## Slide 13

Customized and unique service
The SRO should have a real product match with the SPO! Therefore it is very important to assess candidate SPO’s very thoroughly on their strengths (and weaknesses)
From the SPO’s point of view: use consultative selling instead of traditional selling
Consultative selling: understand the customers business, assess the customers market, list the customers challenges, and map the customers business against its competitors! Services are tailored to the customers need.

## Slide 14

Organisational Alignment
A basic rule about the alignment (match) of the SRO organisation to the SPO organisation:
All levels (hierarchy) must be covered by both organisations
The grouping of tasks and responsibilities should be similar (as much as possible) (e.g process managers (horizontals) on each side, application managers on each side (covering the same applications) business managers (verticals) on both sides etc..)

## Slide 16

How to select an SPO?
Traditionaly organisations are focussed on lowest price.  What really brings value is an SPO that helps to reduce cost, manage complexity and maintain leading exge skills and adopt technological innovations.
A pitfall for SPO’s is: once they gained a reputation of quality and innovation, a huge potential to grow is created. If the focus is then put on growth, these companies lose their innovative spirit!
Always choose innovation over price!
This is often repeated in selection process literature, but how do you exactly deal with innovation in a SMART way?

## Slide 17

Selecting an SPO
An SRO must need to know the strength of their potential SPO. What are you looking for?? Possible competitive differentiators of SPO’s:
Cultural match
Superior client facing skills
Superior business domain skills
High employee availability
An SRO must sort out thoroughly what is important for them and selection should be based on that.

## Slide 18

Some important ‘hygiene’ selection criteria
financial health and stability of the SPO (steady growth and profit).
Employee stability (= continuity of service delivery) e.g. measured by Attrition rate
Somehow an SRO must assess the quality of the available employees (the quality of the bench resources). The best employees are seldom available (‘on the bench’) How does an SPO assure quality?

## Slide 19

Vendor lock-in
= Too much dependency – can’t get rid of the SPO!
It is key to avoid vendor lock-in. How to prevent this?
Perform at least 20% of IT services inhouse
Enable IT staff to spread themselves across all IT functions of the SPO such that knowledge is acquired about the outsourced work
Fill key positions in outsourced projects (architecture, lead designers, lead developers)
