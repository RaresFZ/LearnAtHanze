Practice exam, answers and glossary
What is the difference between Lean and Six Sigma?
Lean emphasizes value, flow and waste reduction; Six Sigma emphasizes defects, variation and process capability.
Name the five Lean principles in order.
Value, value stream, flow, pull, perfection
Which two pillars are associated with TPS?
Just-in-Time and Jidoka.
What are Muda, Mura and Muri?
Waste, unevenness and overburden
What is the difference between motion and transportation waste?
Motion concerns unnecessary movement of people; transportation concerns movement of items/materials/information.
What is the eighth waste in the common eight-waste model?
Unused employee talent or skills.
What is a ninth sustainability waste lens?
Avoidable environmental resource use, emissions or materials; not a universally standard ninth category.
What does SIPOC stand for?
Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers.
What does the S in SIPOC mean?
Suppliers.
What does VOC stand for?
Voice of the Customer.
What is a Champion responsible for?
Sponsoring and aligning improvement work, securing resources and removing barriers.
Which DMAIC phase typically contains the Project Charter?
Define
What does the I in DMAIC mean?
Improve
What is the final DMAIC phase?
Control
What is a tollgate?
A phase-end evidence review and authorization decision
What is the role of a Green Belt?
Leading bounded improvement projects and applying appropriate analytical methods.
What is the role of a White Belt?
Basic awareness of Lean Six Sigma and support for improvement culture.
Is the Master Black Belt a project sponsor?
Not by definition; it is a technical coaching/deployment role, while the Champion is typically the sponsor.
What is Kaizen?
Continuous incremental improvement with staff involvement
What is a Poka Yoke example?
A connector that fits only the correct orientation or a form that rejects invalid input.
Must the 5 Whys method ask exactly five questions?
No; continue until causes are sufficiently evidenced and actionable.
What is an Ishikawa diagram also called?
Fishbone or cause-and-effect diagram.
What is root cause analysis for?
To investigate and verify mechanisms that produce a problem, not merely treat symptoms.
What is the difference between takt time and cycle time?
Takt is demand-based required pace; cycle time is observed process cycle/output interval, depending on definition.
What is lead time?
Elapsed time from request to delivery including waiting.
Calculate takt for 450 min and demand 150.
450/150 = 3 minutes per unit.
What does DPMO mean?
Defects per Million Opportunities.
Compute DPMO: 24 defects, 400 units, 3 opportunities each.
24/(400×3)×1,000,000 = 20,000.
What is First Time Right?
Completing work correctly without rework on the first attempt.
What is a swimlane diagram?
A process map that assigns steps to roles, teams or systems and shows handoffs
What is a Value Stream Map?
A map of end-to-end work and information flows, time and inventory/queues.
What does 5S stand for?
Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain.
What is Kanban?
A visual pull/replenishment and work-in-progress control mechanism.
What is SMED?
A systematic method to reduce changeover time by separating and simplifying internal/external activities
What is TPM?
Total Productive Maintenance.
What does OEE measure?
Availability × Performance × Quality.
Calculate OEE: 0.9 × 0.8 × 0.95.
68.4%
What is Hoshin Kanri?
Deployment and alignment of strategy with operational objectives and reviews.
What is the purpose of a control plan?
To monitor and sustain the improvement with clear owners and response rules.
Is a fishbone diagram proof of root causes?
No; it organizes hypotheses that need verification.
