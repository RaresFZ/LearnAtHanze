Exercise 4 | Corrected answers

1. Takt time

Daily demand = 15,000 / 250 = 60 products. Available time = 8 × 60 − 30 = 450 minutes/day. Takt time = 450 / 60 =
7.50 minutes per product.

2. Cycle times

Output per step = opening stock + inflow − closing stock. Average cycle time = 450 minutes / output. Calculations: Sort
material: 90 + 20 − 25 = 85; Assembling: 95 + 25 − 65 = 55; Apply lacquer layer: 160 + 30 − 130 = 60; Packing and
sending: 125 + 30 − 90 = 65.
3. Which step fails to meet takt time?

Only Assembling: 8.18 minutes per product exceeds the 7.50-minute takt time. It is the capacity bottleneck.

4. What does a cycle time greater than takt time mean?

That process step cannot sustainably keep pace with customer demand at the measured output rate. Backlogs and
delivery delays may arise.

5. Lead time by process step

Using the simplified method specified by the supplied answer key: processing time = cycle time × FTE; waiting
time = cycle time × opening stock; lead time = processing time + waiting time. Figures below are in minutes.

6. Process efficiency

Total processing time = 58.58 minutes. Process efficiency = (58.58083 / 3377.70876) × 100 = 1.73%.

7. Improving process efficiency

Reduce intermediate inventories and waiting time, balance the process steps, address the assembling bottleneck, and
consider pull-based production to improve flow.

Methodological note

These results correct the arithmetic and transcription mistakes in the original answer sheet. The provided lead-time
formula is a classroom simplification, not a universal lead-time law. Actual waiting time depends on the flow of items,
queues and operating conditions. The output figures are inferred from inventory changes; they should not automatically
be interpreted as steady-state production rates.
